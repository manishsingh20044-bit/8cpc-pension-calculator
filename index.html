Where the 8th Pay Commission stands today

The Union Cabinet approved the Terms of Reference of the 8th Central Pay Commission on 28 October 2025, and the Commission was formally constituted on 3 November 2025 under the chairmanship of Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai. It has 18 months from that date to make its recommendations, which puts the report at around May 2027. Since then it has collected memorandums (submissions closed on 15 June 2026) and held consultations in cities including Jaipur, Chandigarh, Bengaluru and Mumbai.

As of 28 October 2026 no fitment factor, new pension matrix or date of effect has been announced. The Government's press release said that, going by the usual ten-year cycle, the effect of the recommendations "would normally be expected from 01.01.2026" — a statement of past practice, not a decision. Every number this calculator produces is therefore an estimate built on assumptions that you can change.

How the calculator works

The 7th Pay Commission revised pensions by multiplying the existing basic pension by a single fitment factor (2.57) and rounding up to the next rupee. Dearness Relief then started again from zero on the new pension. This calculator applies the same method to your current 7th CPC basic pension:

  1. Your pension today = basic pension + additional pension for age (if 80 or older) + Dearness Relief (currently 60% of basic plus additional pension).
  2. New basic pension = current basic pension × fitment factor, rounded up. If the result falls below an estimated new minimum (₹9,000 × the same factor), it is raised to that minimum.
  3. New total = new basic + new additional pension (same percentage for your age) + DR on the new pension (assumed 0% at the start).
  4. Arrears = monthly increase × number of months from the assumed date of effect to the month before the revised pension is first paid.

Because DR is already 60%, a factor needs to be above 1.60 just for your total pension to stay the same once DR resets. That is why the comparison table shows a much smaller jump in total pension than the factor alone suggests. At 2.57, ₹9,000 basic becomes ₹23,130 — but today's ₹9,000 pension already pays ₹14,400 with DR, so the real monthly gain is ₹8,730 (about 61%).

Which fitment factor should you pick?

Nobody knows the final figure yet. Use 2.57 to see what a repeat of the 7th CPC would mean, 3.833 to see the demand made by the staff side of the National Council (JCM) and pensioner associations, and something in between for a middle case. Employee bodies' demands range widely — one railway association has even proposed graded factors up to 4.38 for the top pay levels — while the lowest figures quoted in the media are around 1.92. The fitment factor calculator explains each number, and the pension chart lists ready-made results from ₹9,000 to ₹1,25,000.

What the estimate does not include

  • Commutation: if you commuted part of your pension, the monthly deduction continues until restoration. Enter your full basic pension, not the reduced amount credited. If you only know what reaches your bank, the basic pension finder works it out.
  • Income tax, Fixed Medical Allowance and CGHS: not part of the calculation.
  • Changes in DR before implementation: if a July 2026 or January 2027 DR increase is announced, your current pension rises and the arrears change. Update the DR box to reflect it, or use the DA/DR calculator for the DR arrears alone.
  • Special rules the Commission may recommend: for example a different method for older pensioners (the 7th CPC gave pre-2016 pensioners the higher of two methods), a new minimum pension or changed age slabs. When official numbers arrive this site will be updated. Our formulas and sources are on How we calculate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fitment factor for pensioners in the 8th Pay Commission?

It has not been announced. As of 28 October 2026 the Commission has not submitted its report. The 7th CPC used 2.57; the NC-JCM staff side and pensioner bodies have asked for 3.833. Figures like 1.92, 2.28 or 2.86 are estimates, not official numbers.

How much will a ₹9,000 pension become under the 8th Pay Commission?

At a factor of 2.57 it would become ₹23,130; at 2.86, ₹25,740; at 3.0, ₹27,000; at 3.833, ₹34,497. These are estimates. Remember that DR (now 60%) is expected to restart from zero, so the gain in total pension is smaller than the jump in basic pension.

When will the 8th Pay Commission be implemented for pensioners?

The date has not been fixed. The Commission's report is due within 18 months of 3 November 2025, around May 2027. The Government has said the effect would normally be expected from 1 January 2026 based on past practice, which is why arrears are commonly estimated from that date.

Will pensioners get arrears from January 2026?

If the Government sets 1 January 2026 as the date of effect, as happened with the 7th CPC (effective 1 January 2016, paid from mid-2016), pensioners would get arrears for the gap. This is not yet decided. The calculator lets you change both the date of effect and the payment month.

Is Dearness Relief included in the new pension?

Past pay commissions merged the DR into the new basic pension through the fitment factor and restarted DR at 0%. The calculator assumes the same. You can see DR today (60%) and after revision side by side.

Is this calculator official?

No. It is an independent tool and is not affiliated with the Government of India, DoPPW, CPAO or the 8th Pay Commission. Every result is an estimate. Your actual revised pension will be fixed by your Pension Disbursing Authority under official orders.
