What is a fitment factor?

A fitment factor is a single number by which the old basic pay or basic pension is multiplied to fix the new one when a pay commission is implemented. For pensioners the method under the 7th CPC was simple: the pension being drawn on 31 December 2015 was multiplied by 2.57 and rounded up to the next rupee. That one multiplier did two jobs at once — it absorbed the Dearness Relief that had built up (125% at the time) and added a real increase on top.

Because the 8th CPC has not reported, there is no official fitment factor yet. The table below explains where the commonly quoted numbers come from, so you can choose sensible scenarios instead of believing a viral figure.

The break-even factor: why 1.63 matters

Dearness Relief is 63% of basic pension from 1 July 2026 (Cabinet decision of 28 October 2026). If DR resets to zero when the new pension starts, a fitment factor of exactly 1.63 would leave your total monthly pension unchanged. Only the part of the factor above 1.63 is a real increase. For a ₹20,000 basic pension:

  • Today: ₹20,000 + 63% DR (₹12,600) = ₹32,600
  • At 1.92: ₹38,400 → an increase of ₹5,800 (about 18%)
  • At 2.57: ₹51,400 → an increase of ₹18,800 (about 58%)
  • At 2.86: ₹57,200 → an increase of ₹24,600 (about 75%)
  • At 3.833: ₹76,660 → an increase of ₹44,060 (about 135%)

If another DR instalment is announced before implementation (the next would normally take effect from 1 January 2027), the break-even point moves up with it. Change the DR box in the calculator to see the effect.

How the 8th CPC is likely to arrive at its factor

Historically the factor has been worked out from a proposed minimum pay. The 7th CPC fixed the minimum pay at ₹18,000 against ₹7,000 earlier, a ratio of 2.57, and applied it uniformly. Employee bodies use the same logic for their demands: the NC-JCM staff side's proposed minimum pay of ₹69,000 divided by ₹18,000 gives 3.833. The Commission's Terms of Reference ask it to keep in view fiscal prudence, the unfunded cost of non-contributory pension schemes and the impact on state finances, which is why most analysts expect a final figure well below union demands. One railway supervisors' association has suggested graded factors (2.92 for lower levels up to 4.38 for the highest), but a single uniform factor for pensioners has been the norm so far.

Using the reverse calculator

If a news report says "minimum pension may rise to ₹25,740", enter ₹9,000 and ₹25,740 in the reverse box above: the implied factor is 2.86. This is a quick way to check whether a headline figure is consistent with a known factor, or simply made up.

Tips for reading fitment factor news

  • Check whether a figure is a demand, an estimate or an order. Only a Cabinet decision on pib.gov.in followed by a DoPPW/DoE order is official.
  • A higher basic does not mean the same percentage rise in take-home pension, because DR resets.
  • For pre-2016 and post-2016 pensioners the Commission may recommend different methods (the 7th CPC also offered a notional pay-fixation option). Treat any single-factor result as a first estimate.

Common mistakes with fitment factors

  • Applying the factor to the total pension. It applies to basic pension only.
  • Comparing new basic with old basic. Compare the new basic with today's basic plus DR — that is what you actually receive.
  • Assuming one factor for everyone. The Commission could recommend different methods for older pensioners or for different levels, as some associations have asked.
  • Trusting "confirmed" factors on social media. As of 30 October 2026 there is none.

Frequently asked questions

Has the 8th Pay Commission fitment factor been announced?

No. As of 30 October 2026 no fitment factor has been announced. The Commission's report is expected around May 2027.

What fitment factor did the 7th Pay Commission use for pensioners?

2.57. Pension drawn on 31.12.2015 was multiplied by 2.57 and rounded up to the next rupee, with DR restarting at zero from 1 January 2016.

Why do unions demand 3.833?

The NC-JCM staff side has proposed a minimum pay of ₹69,000. Divided by the current minimum pay of ₹18,000, that gives 3.833. Pensioner associations have backed the same figure.

What fitment factor keeps my pension the same?

With DR at 63%, a factor of 1.63 would leave the total unchanged if DR resets to zero. Any factor above that is a real increase.

Is 2.86 the confirmed fitment factor?

No. 2.86 is one of several estimates in media reports. It has no official basis as of 30 October 2026.

What is the formula for the fitment factor?

Fitment factor = new basic pension ÷ old basic pension. Historically it has been derived from the proposed minimum pay: the 7th CPC raised minimum pay from ₹7,000 to ₹18,000, a ratio of 2.57.

Is the fitment factor the same for pensioners and employees?

Under the 7th CPC the same 2.57 multiplier was used for employees' pay and for pre-2016 pensioners' pension (with a notional-pay alternative for pensioners). The 8th CPC's approach is not known yet.

How much will pension increase at a fitment factor of 2.86?

Basic pension rises 2.86 times. Because DR (63%) restarts at zero, the total monthly pension rises by about 75% — for example, ₹20,000 basic goes from ₹32,600 today to ₹57,200.

Why do media reports quote 1.92 or 2.28?

These are analysts' estimates based on assumptions about inflation and fiscal space. They are not recommendations of the Commission or decisions of the Government.

Can the fitment factor be lower than 2.57?

Yes, it is possible. The Terms of Reference ask the Commission to keep fiscal prudence and the unfunded cost of pensions in view. Any factor above about 1.63 (with DR at 63%) still increases the total pension.

When will the fitment factor be announced?

Only after the Commission submits its report (due around May 2027) and the Government decides on it. The 7th CPC reported in November 2015 and the Cabinet decided in June 2016.
